A lot of AI conversations still begin with the same question:

**How many people can this replace?**

It is understandable.

Headcount is visible.
Cost is measurable.
Automation promises efficiency.

But it is often the wrong first question.

A more useful one is:

**If a machine starts making part of the decision, how should the surrounding operating model change?**

I saw this problem years before generative AI became part of everyday business conversation.

At Amazon, we were working with machine-learning interventions across operational processes.

Two measures mattered.

The first was accuracy.

When the model made a decision, how often was it correct?

The second was coverage or efficiency.

How much of the total workload could the model actually handle?

These are not the same thing.

A model can be highly accurate and still create very little operational value if it only handles a tiny percentage of the work.

Equally, a model can process large volumes and create unacceptable risk if the audit and escalation model around it is weak.

This is where the operating model enters the conversation.

New models needed stronger human oversight initially.

As confidence improved, the audit percentage could fall.

People had to learn how to review machine outputs.

The exception path had to be clear.

Someone had to own model errors.

Complex cases still required judgement.

And employees doing repetitive operational work needed a pathway toward different work: auditing, exceptions, investigation or higher-complexity decisions.

The technology changed.

So the work had to change with it.

This is why I am cautious when organisations describe AI transformation primarily as a technology rollout.

The model itself is only one layer.

You also need to redesign:

decision rights

human oversight

exception management

quality controls

training

accountability

performance measurement

and sometimes the organisation itself.

<div class="pull-quote"><p>AI is not simply inserted into an operating model. It changes the operating model.</p></div>

And in many cases, that is where most of the value and most of the difficulty sit.
