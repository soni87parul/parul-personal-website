problem: "Addverb was scaling quickly across functions and geographies. I joined Special Initiatives and the CEO Office to help the Global CEO understand where execution was beginning to break as the organisation grew. At first, the symptoms looked disconnected: procurement inefficiencies, changing designs, inventory problems, manufacturing delays, fragmented after-sales support and systems that did not seem to talk to each other. They weren't disconnected."
why_it_mattered: ""
figure_out: "I started with leadership KPIs, conversations with CXOs and teams, and the operating data behind them. One clue became particularly important: there was no reliable single Bill of Materials for Zippy, one of the company's flagship robots. Design teams could be working from one version while Procurement was buying against another. Changes moved through emails and individual conversations. Inventory planning suffered. Manufacturing lacked a reliable flow of approved design information. After-sales teams often had to pull resources back from Design and Manufacturing to solve customer issues. What initially looked like several departmental problems was fundamentally an information and operating-system problem."
design: "Rather than treating ERP implementation as an IT project, we mapped the AS-IS processes across functions and identified manual interventions, gaps, duplication and broken hand-offs. Then came the TO-BE architecture. The solution involved an integrated technology and process environment spanning ERP, PLM and after-sales workflows, with functional leaders, SMEs, vendors and the CEO/CXO team involved in designing the future state."
build: "The implementation involved vendor selection, process redesign, multiple rounds of alpha and beta testing, departmental UAT, system-integration testing and pilot implementation. But some of the harder work was human. Changing systems meant changing ownership, workflows and long-established ways of working. Senior stakeholders disagreed. Teams worried about operational disruption. Some processes had to be overhauled while the business continued running. My job increasingly became connecting the CEO's transformation ambition with the people who actually had to make the new operating model work."
scale: "Before implementation, teams were trained on the new environment. SOPs were documented. Financial and inventory data were migrated. Functional teams and vendors remained involved through the stabilisation period. The objective wasn't simply to launch software. It was to leave behind a system the organisation could operate without depending on tribal knowledge and disconnected decision-making."
transform: "The transformation took approximately 22 months and involved roughly ₹15 Cr of investment. The work addressed an approximately ₹90 Cr savings opportunity, including significant inventory inefficiencies, while integrating Design, ERP and manufacturing systems toward a single source of truth."
