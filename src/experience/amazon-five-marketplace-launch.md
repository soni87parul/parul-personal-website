problem: "I was given the mandate to launch Restricted Products Compliance and Dangerous Goods operations for five new and emerging Amazon marketplaces. The programme had already lost roughly two months before it reached me, but the expected launch window had not moved. At the same time, I needed to hire and train new teams while dealing with a substantial backlog of product listings that still required review."
why_it_mattered: "The launch could not simply be delayed until hiring was complete. Compliance operations had to be ready when the marketplaces went live, and speed could not come at the cost of weakening restricted-products or dangerous-goods controls. The real problem was capacity creation under a compressed timeline."
figure_out: "I separated the work into parallel constraints: immediate review capacity, hiring, training, backlog clearance and launch readiness. Instead of waiting for the future team to arrive before work could begin, I looked for capacity already available inside the organisation and for parts of the backlog that could simultaneously become training material."
design: "The operating plan used several levers at once. Existing teams absorbed additional volume where possible. Hiring requirements were aligned with adjacent roles so people could be transferred or recruited faster. SMEs were organised into a training layer, and live operational volume was used to train new hires while experienced reviewers audited accuracy. I also looked for repetitive portions of the workflow that could be handled through bulk actions, RPA or machine-led interventions rather than asking people to solve the entire backlog manually."
build: "Hiring, training and operations therefore ran concurrently rather than sequentially. Existing teams, transferred talent, trainers, SMEs and new hires worked against the same launch plan. The backlog became both operational work and a controlled training environment, while automation was applied where the risk profile allowed it."
scale: "This approach supported the launch of five marketplaces under the compressed programme and became part of a broader pattern in my Amazon work: when growth creates a capacity problem, the answer is often a combination of people, process redesign and technology rather than simply adding headcount."
