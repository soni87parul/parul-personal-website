problem: "In 2014, Indian e-commerce was growing rapidly and logistics was becoming part of the customer proposition. At Snapdeal, we had already reduced the broader last-mile timeline from around six days to approximately 72 hours. But the data suggested that wasn't enough."
why_it_mattered: ""
figure_out: "I looked at sales data, complaints, items added to cart, category behaviour, market size and what competitors were doing. A pattern emerged. For certain high-value categories (particularly products such as mobile phones and luxury items), customers valued speed disproportionately. The question changed from: How do we make every delivery faster? to: Where would speed change customer behaviour enough to justify a different operating model?"
design: "We built the model around three choices. First, work with manufacturers or large vendors capable of supporting the service. Second, restrict the proposition initially to pin codes where the last mile could be tightly controlled and integrated with Snapdeal's technology. Third, create dedicated OneShip capability for priority Same-Day and Next-Day orders."
build: "The solution wasn't simply a new delivery SLA. It required vendor agreements, inventory positioning, fulfilment logic, selected geographies and technology workflows capable of recognising and prioritising eligible orders."
scale: "Once the model worked, SOPs and frameworks were developed and the approach became connected to the broader fulfilment model. The work included Same-Day/Next-Day rollout across metro delivery hubs and the structural reduction of delivery lead times from approximately six days to 72 hours."
