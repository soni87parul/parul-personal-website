problem: "As we started speaking with investors and potential corporate customers about cultivated meat, one question kept appearing: When can you actually sell it? That question exposed a much bigger problem."

figure_out: "Food products require regulatory approval. As engagement around the regulatory pathway developed, the underlying issue became clearer: the challenge wasn't merely getting one company's product licensed. India needed a regulatory framework capable of dealing with the category itself. That moved the problem from company strategy into ecosystem strategy."
design: "The work expanded into discussions involving regulatory stakeholders, scientists, government ministries, industry participants and companies. Questions went beyond one licence: How should cultivated products be classified? What manufacturing standards should apply? How should ingredients be treated? How should such products be packaged and communicated to consumers? What could India learn from regulatory approaches elsewhere?"
build: "We participated in industry discussions, developed supporting material, compared international approaches and continued engaging stakeholders around the broader alternative-protein ecosystem. This eventually expanded beyond regulation. If India wanted companies in this sector to succeed, they would also need access to research infrastructure, manufacturing capability, expertise and startup support."
scale: "That thinking contributed to work around an alternative-protein excellence-facility concept involving institutional/government stakeholders and an ecosystem model incorporating research, training, manufacturing and startup support."
transform: "This work remains ongoing. The point of this story is recognising that commercialisation depended on a system that did not yet exist, and beginning to work on the system itself."
