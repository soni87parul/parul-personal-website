problem: "A culturally sensitive catalogue incident showed that conventional restricted-product controls were not enough for every kind of risk. Listings that were offensive in an Indian cultural context could appear in international marketplaces without containing the obvious keywords that traditional rule-based systems depended on. The immediate issue required containment, but the larger question was how to detect similar products systematically in the future."
why_it_mattered: "This was not just a catalogue-cleanup problem. Cultural risk does not always map neatly to a global keyword list, and relying entirely on manual knowledge would make the response slow, inconsistent and difficult to scale across international teams."
figure_out: "We first created a manual review response around listings already identified through social channels and similar catalogue patterns. That helped contain the immediate issue, but it also exposed the limitation of the existing approach: the same cultural symbol can appear as a photograph, illustration, animation or stylised image, often with little useful text attached to it. The problem therefore needed to move from keyword recognition toward visual characteristics."
design: "I worked with the ML team exploring reverse-image and image-selection capabilities and suggested focusing the model on recurring visual features rather than trying to feed it every possible image of a deity. Distinctive objects, forms and associated visual characteristics could help narrow the catalogue pool for review. The technical mechanism was paired with a governance response: similar listings could be bundled through seller or upload relationships, while cultural restrictions could be made clearer for sellers and reviewers."
build: "The team combined image-led identification, automated bundling and human review. We also developed cultural guidance and seller education so the response was not limited to detection after a listing appeared."
scale: "The approach created a repeatable catalogue-level mechanism for surfacing potentially sensitive listings and supported a more structured seller-training model. Human judgement remained important, but technology reduced dependence on every reviewer possessing the same cultural knowledge."
transform: "The lasting change was moving from incident-by-incident manual containment toward a combination of cultural intelligence, image-based technology, seller education and governance."
