problem: "Within UN Trade and Development's eTrade for Women work, physical masterclasses were being used to build investment readiness and business capability among women entrepreneurs. In discussions with the programme team, it became clear that physical sessions alone could not create the reach, continuity or repeatable support infrastructure the programme aspired to build."
why_it_mattered: "Capacity-building is difficult to scale when knowledge is delivered mainly through individual events. Entrepreneurs need access to structured learning over time, while the wider ecosystem also needs mechanisms that connect capability-building with market access and business-support services."
figure_out: "I started with the programme's global ambition and the gap between that ambition and the output of a primarily physical model. The issue was not simply “more training”. The programme needed a digital infrastructure that could combine learning with practical pathways into markets and support."
design: "I proposed a broader architecture with three connected elements: an LMS for structured learning, a marketplace layer that could connect corporates with women-led businesses, and a business-support-services engine through which entrepreneurs could access curated professional support. For the LMS, I designed the learning framework around investment readiness, forms and sources of funding, equity, financial fundamentals and compliance."
build: "A digital agency was engaged to develop the LMS. I acted as the subject-matter and programme architect, building the theme, expanding the content into modules and working with the agency through the development and audit process."
scale: "The significance of the model is that the knowledge architecture can move beyond one physical masterclass at a time and become a reusable digital capacity layer. The marketplace and BSS components were conceived to extend that capacity-building into commercial and operating support."
