problem: "Finance was reviewing leakage across the business and Supply Chain carried roughly ₹30 million of losses on the balance sheet. Some of the loss could be traced to last-mile and warehouse operations, but a large portion remained unexplained. Internally, it had effectively become a supply-chain “black box”. I was asked to find out what was actually sitting behind that number."
why_it_mattered: "This was not simply an accounting exercise. Until we understood whether the losses came from logistics partners, warehousing, inventory records, financial reconciliation or seller behaviour, the business could not assign ownership or stop the leakage from recurring."
figure_out: "I broke the loss into individual streams and followed the transactions rather than treating it as one aggregate number. In last mile, I reviewed returns, RTOs and damaged or missing shipments by vendor and matched complaints back to delivery behaviour and contract terms. In warehousing, I looked at product age, inventory movement and vendor liability. That investigation exposed a deeper anomaly: physical inventory and financial reconciliation were not always moving together when products were returned. Going further into six months of warehouse ledgers, we found cases where the same returned inventory had effectively been reconciled multiple times financially while the stock itself was still present in the warehouse. What looked like a supply-chain loss was partly a system and reconciliation-design problem."
design: "Once the pattern was clear, I brought Account Management, Technology, Finance, Seller Support and warehouse teams into the same investigation. We separated genuine logistics losses from reconciliation errors and created a seller-by-seller recovery approach using financial reconciliation sheets, GRNs, inventory evidence and contractual responsibility. In parallel, we defined the need for an automated reconciliation mechanism that could connect physical warehouse inventory with the financial ledger."
build: "The recovery programme was run nationally in phases, starting with sellers carrying the highest outstanding amounts. Teams worked through records, met sellers, reviewed proofs, handled disputes and negotiated settlements. In parallel, I worked with the Unicommerce/technology side to move toward an automated seller-reconciliation system for fulfilment-model vendors."
