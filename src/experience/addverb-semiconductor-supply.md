problem: "A major customer project was running into serious delay because the required sorting robots were not ready. When the issue reached the Global CEO, I was asked to understand what was actually blocking delivery. Discussions with Manufacturing and Design traced the problem to semiconductor availability for the robots' vision systems. The company had placed chip orders, but the specifications had changed and replacement supply was caught in the global semiconductor shortage."
why_it_mattered: "Treating this as an emergency purchase for one project would still leave the company exposed. Procurement lead times were uncertain, project penalties and budgets were increasing, and several other customer projects would soon require related chip categories."
figure_out: "I reviewed the upcoming project portfolio rather than looking only at the delayed project. Across the following roughly 28 months, there were multiple deliveries requiring a small number of recurring chip categories. Aggregating that demand changed the commercial position: what looked like a small emergency order could become a strategic supply conversation."
design: "With the CEO, I moved the discussion toward a longer-horizon supplier relationship. Instead of negotiating each requirement independently, we approached Infineon around combined demand, future project visibility and a broader technology relationship."
build: "The discussion progressed from supply availability into business planning. During meetings around LOGIMAT in Germany, the conversation covered the near-term chip requirement as well as a longer strategic partnership."
scale: "The resulting direction was a multi-year supply relationship rather than repeated project-level buying, together with discussions around longer-term manufacturing capability and technology transfer."
