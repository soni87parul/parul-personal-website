problem: "This was 2018. AI and machine learning were nowhere near as visible in everyday business conversations as they are today, but within Amazon we were already exploring how machine-led workflows could increase operational capacity. The question I was interested in was not simply: Can a machine do this work? It was: How do you introduce machine decision-making into a live operation without losing control of accuracy, risk and people?"
why_it_mattered: ""
figure_out: "We were working with three different ML approaches across processes, including image-based and review-volume use cases. Two measures mattered enormously. Accuracy: when the model made a decision, how often was it right? Efficiency/coverage: how much of the total operational volume could it actually handle? A model could be highly accurate but commercially irrelevant if it could only process a tiny fraction of the work."
design: "We designed a phased audit mechanism. New models began with high human oversight. As performance stabilised and the error delta reduced, the percentage of work requiring human audit could progressively fall. At the same time, employees had to learn how to operate alongside the models, audit outputs and handle the complex cases automation couldn't resolve."
build: "This became an important part of the transformation. Rather than viewing automation simply as headcount reduction, people could move away from repetitive operational work toward auditing, exception management and more complex cases. The technology and the operating model evolved together."
scale: "By 2020, three models had been implemented across three different processes, with varying levels of efficiency while maintaining approximately 85% accuracy in the examples documented in the experience inventory."
transform: "Together, the interventions created operational capacity equivalent to approximately 130+ FTEs, capacity that could support marketplace launches and business expansion."
